In the Phoenix pipeline, **Generalized Algebraic Theories (GATs)** provide the formal backbone: each specification becomes a GAT instance (a *theory*) that declares operations—like `createTask` or `archiveTask`—along with their types and arities. These theories don't exist in isolation; each Implementation Unit (IU) is essentially a GAT fragment describing one domain's behavior. When the pipeline reaches the codegen phase, it faces the problem of merging these fragments into a single coherent deliverable. This is where **colimits** come in: the colimit operation takes multiple GATs (the IUs) and computes their "pushout"—a single unified theory that contains all operations from all fragments, with overlapping concepts automatically identified and merged. Just as the rational numbers are the colimit of all fractions with the same value, the generated deliverable is the colimit of all IU theories that share common structure. The result is a working system where `Task` appears exactly once even when referenced by Archive, Priority, and Status IUs—each operation preserved, nothing duplicated, guaranteed consistent by the universal property of the colimit.
